How to Choose an AVR for Your TV

An automatic voltage regulator (AVR) can help when power fluctuates, but the correct model depends on your equipment, load and voltage conditions. Learn what to check before shopping.

Do you need an AVR for your television?

An automatic voltage regulator adjusts its output when incoming voltage changes within its supported operating range. It can be useful where supply voltage varies. It is not the same as a UPS: a typical AVR does not power your TV when electricity fails. Many modern TVs already accept a broad voltage range, so first check your TV's rating label and manufacturer instructions.

AVR watts vs VA: sizing correctly

Read the AVR's continuous rated output in watts (W), if specified, and compare it with the combined input-power ratings of the devices you intend to connect. If a product is rated only in volt-amperes (VA), do not assume that number equals watts; the usable wattage depends on the power factor and manufacturer rating.

Servo AVR or electronic AVR?

Servo types use a motor-driven mechanism to adjust voltage and may respond differently from electronic relay or solid-state designs. Neither type is automatically best for every device. Compare the actual output accuracy, speed, noise, duty rating and manufacturer documentation instead of buying based on the label alone.

Safe setup and common mistakes

  • Do not overload an AVR with a television, amplifier, refrigerator or other devices beyond its published capacity.
  • Never block ventilation, bypass a protective fuse or modify plugs and wiring.
  • If outlets spark, become hot or smell burnt, disconnect the equipment safely and consult a qualified professional.
  • A surge protector, an AVR and a UPS serve different purposes; some products combine features but do not assume all do.
JMAGS tip: Tell the seller your TV model and rated power before choosing an AVR. If the TV frequently resets or has a power fault, voltage fluctuation is only one possible cause; the unit may need diagnosis.

AVR questions

Does an AVR keep a TV running during a brownout?

No. An AVR regulates voltage; a UPS with a charged battery is designed to supply temporary backup power.

Is a 1,000 VA AVR always good for a 1,000 W load?

No. The watts and VA ratings are not interchangeable. Check the device's specified watt capacity.

UPS Buying Guide for TVs, Wi-Fi and PCs

A UPS provides battery backup for a limited time during a power interruption. The best choice depends on the watt load, battery capacity, compatibility and how many minutes you need.

What does a UPS actually do?

A uninterruptible power supply (UPS) is a battery-backed device that can briefly power compatible connected equipment when mains power fails. Some UPS models also provide automatic voltage regulation and surge features. A UPS is usually meant to give you time to save work or safely shut down — not to run a large TV for hours.

VA, watts and runtime explained

Check both the VA rating and the watt output limit of the UPS. Add up the actual watt consumption of your TV, modem, router or PC, including any devices you plan to connect. Real backup duration also depends on battery capacity, age, efficiency, load and operating conditions.

Why does output waveform matter?

Some power supplies need or work best with pure sine wave output when switched to battery mode; others tolerate simulated sine wave. Check your equipment manual and the UPS manufacturer's compatibility guidance before purchasing. A UPS that has enough VA on paper may still be unsuitable for a particular device.

Checklist before buying a UPS

  1. Record your equipment model numbers and rated watts.
  2. Decide how long you actually need backup power.
  3. Check both VA and watt output limits and published runtime charts at your approximate load.
  4. Confirm battery replacement options, battery warranty, connector types and plug compatibility.
  5. Buy from a credible seller with clear safety documentation and after-sales support.
Safety reminder: A UPS contains a battery and high-voltage components. Do not open, repair or modify it without appropriate professional training. If swollen, hot, damaged or leaking, stop using it and seek qualified service.

UPS questions

Can a UPS run my TV all night?

Ordinary compact UPS units generally are not intended for hours of TV use. Calculate the required runtime using the specific model's load chart.

Are 650 VA and 650 W the same?

No. VA measures apparent power; watts describe real power. Many 650 VA UPS units have a lower rated watt output.

Do I still need an AVR?

Some UPS models include voltage regulation; check the exact specification. See our AVR buying guide.
